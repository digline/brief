A river delta forms where a river meets a body of slower water, such as a sea
or a lake. As the current slows, it can no longer carry all the sediment it has
picked up upstream, and the heaviest grains settle first, close to the mouth.

Over many years these deposits build up into low banks, and the river splits
into several channels that wander across them. Each channel carries water and
sediment until it silts up, and the flow then shifts to a new path.

The shape of a delta depends on the balance between the sediment the river
delivers and the waves and tides that remove it. Where the river dominates, the
delta grows outward in long fingers; where waves dominate, it is smoothed.
